\paragraph{Question and status.}
We test whether restricting a local Hessian prior to a later part of a
noise-to-conformer conditional path improves generated physical quality.
The path below is an experimentally unvalidated extension of Alternative A.
It does not infer transition states, basin connectivity, equilibrium weights,
or a global energy landscape from one Hessian.
Taylor disagreement away from the reference does not invalidate CFM;
locality is a proposed inductive bias to be tested, not a condition of the
CFM marginalization theorem.

\section{Inherited conventions}
The archived note \texttt{StifFlow\_v1\_Alternative\_A..tex}, body dated
16 September 2026, supplies configuration space, boundary conditions,
objective, aligned factor and supervision (labels \texttt{eq:space},
\texttt{eq:boundary}, \texttt{eq:objective}, \texttt{eq:offlineL},
\texttt{eq:coding}). This note changes only the conditional path.
Coordinates are centered, non-mass-weighted Cartesian coordinates in
$\mathcal X_G$, with orthonormal basis $Q\in\R^{3N\times(3N-3)}$.
Rotations remain in this linear space, are treated geometrically in the
physical label, and are never identified with reflections.
Flow time is dimensionless, not physical time. The acceleration objective
is a geometric path criterion, not a Newtonian action or a kinetic model.

Let $S=C_0^{1/2}$ be the graph-only symmetric source factor,
$C_K=\varepsilon_E K^{-1}$ the original regularized energetic covariance,
and
\begin{equation}
 L=T_KS=S^{-1}(SC_KS)^{1/2},\qquad
 T_K=S^{-1}(SC_KS)^{1/2}S^{-1},\qquad LL^\top=C_K.
 \label{eq:aligned}
\end{equation}
No commutation of $S$ and $C_K$ is assumed. Original target coordinates are
not replaced by relaxed MMFF94 references. The pair $(x_1,K)$ is sampled
independently of $z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$. The same $z$ and the same $K$ are
used for every time on one conditional path.

\section{A single global intervention time}
Choose $0\leq\tau<1$ globally from a frozen training-only diagnostic, and set
\begin{align}
 \alpha(t)&=t^2(3-2t),\qquad
 u_\tau(t)=\max\{0,(t-\tau)/(1-\tau)\},\nonumber\\
 g_\tau(t)&=(1-t)u_\tau(t)^2,\qquad
 \dot g_\tau(t)=
 \begin{cases}0,&t\leq\tau,\\2u_\tau-3u_\tau^2,&t>\tau.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:gate}
\end{align}
The factor and sample supervision are
\begin{align}
 A_\tau(t)&=(1-t)S+g_\tau(t)(L-S),\label{eq:factor}\\
 X_t&=\alpha(t)x_1+QA_\tau(t)z,\nonumber\\
 U_t&=\dot\alpha(t)x_1+
 Q[-S+\dot g_\tau(t)(L-S)]z.\label{eq:target}
\end{align}
At $\tau=0$, $g_0=t^2(1-t)$ and this is exactly the archived $A_E$.
For $t\leq\tau$, it is exactly the stiffness-free smoothstep control.
At the two endpoints,
\begin{equation}
 X_0=QSz,\quad U_0=-QSz,\quad X_1=x_1,\quad U_1=-QLz.
 \label{eq:endpoints}
\end{equation}
The path is $C^1$ and piecewise cubic, with a finite jump of the second
derivative at $\tau>0$. It belongs to $H^2$; no impulse in acceleration is
introduced. Derivative checks at the knot must account for the first-order
central-difference error there.
Numerically, for $t>\tau$, evaluate the $S$ coefficient as
$(1-t)^2(1+u_\tau)/(1-\tau)$ and its derivative as
$-(1-t)(1+3u_\tau)/(1-\tau)$. These equivalent expressions avoid cancellation
of source-sized terms in terminal hard modes; no endpoint truncation is used.

\begin{proposition}[Interior density and endpoint calibration]
For $t<1$, $A_\tau(t)$ is invertible. Its normalized endpoint covariance
equals the original $C_K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Writing $\chi=u_\tau^2\in[0,1]$ gives
$A_\tau=(1-t)[(1-\chi)I+\chi T_K]S$.
The bracket is SPD because $T_K$ is SPD, without commuting any factors.
As $t\uparrow1$, $\chi\to1$, whence $A_\tau/(1-t)\to L$ and
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\uparrow1}\frac{A_\tau A_\tau^\top}{(1-t)^2}=C_K.
 \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
Also $(\alpha-1)/(1-t)\to0$.
\end{proof}
This is a statement about conditional supervision. It is neither a
prediction for learned endpoint residual covariance nor evidence that GEOM
conformer frequencies follow a harmonic Boltzmann distribution.

\section{Constrained acceleration optimum and its price}
Fix the original source, target, endpoint velocities and aligned terminal
factor in \eqref{eq:endpoints}. Require every sample path to equal the
control on $[0,\tau]$. Among $H^2$ continuations on $[\tau,1]$, the unique
minimum of the squared-acceleration objective is \eqref{eq:target}.
Indeed, the Euler--Lagrange equation on the free interval is $X^{(4)}=0$.
The four position/velocity conditions at $\tau$ and $1$ give the cubic
Hermite continuation in \eqref{eq:factor}. For a competing perturbation $h$
with $h=\dot h=0$ at both ends, integration by parts gives
$\int\langle\ddot X,\ddot h\rangle=0$, so the action increment is
$\int\norm{\ddot h}^2$, strictly positive for nonzero $h$.

With $\ell=1-\tau$, $D=L-S$ and $u=(t-\tau)/\ell$, the noise acceleration
on the free interval is $Q(2-6u)Dz/\ell$. Independence and $\E z=0$ give
\begin{equation}
 J_\tau=\E_z\int_0^1\norm{\ddot X_t}^2dt
 =12\norm{x_1}^2+\frac4{1-\tau}\norm{L-S}_F^2.
 \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
Optimizing a terminal covariance factor under $LL^\top=C_K$ still minimizes
$\norm{L-S}_F^2$; the positive multiplier $4/\ell$ leaves the original
Gaussian--Bures alignment \eqref{eq:aligned} unchanged. This invokes the
established alignment result used by Alternative A, not a new OT theorem.

The total squared supervision difference from the control is
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1\E_z\norm{U_t-U_t^{\rm control}}^2dt
 =\frac2{15}(1-\tau)\norm{L-S}_F^2.
 \label{eq:signal}
\end{equation}
The identities follow from
$\int_0^1(2-6u)^2du=4$ and
$\int_0^1(2u-3u^2)^2du=2/15$.
Relative to $\tau=0$, delaying increases the noise action by $1/\ell$ and
reduces this signal integral by $\ell$. At $\tau=1/2$ these are $2$ and
$1/2$. No lower approximation error, faster integration, or stronger
stiffness supervision follows. Timing and total signal strength are
confounded in this experiment.

\section{CFM, symmetry and the matched null}
Use ordinary Euclidean CFM, uniform interior training time, unchanged
backbone and sampler, and only $(x_t,t,G)$ as neural input. A global proper
Haar rotation acts jointly on the sample coordinates and velocities after
constructing the target. Stiffness does not enter inference inputs.

For the null, fix one independent relative proper rotation $R$ along a
conditional path and let $W=Q^\top(I_N\otimes R)Q$. The graph source is
spatially isotropic, hence $WS=SW$. Replace $L$ by $WLW^\top$ while retaining
the target coordinates, then apply the same global Haar augmentation.
The null factors satisfy $A_\tau^{\rm null}=WA_\tau W^\top$ and likewise
for $\dot A_\tau$. Thus covariance spectra and expected squared sample-noise
velocities match at every time. This null disrupts relative spatial
orientation; it is not arbitrary internal-mode randomization.

\section{Experimental interpretation}
The frozen protocol compares existing A and A rot-null with at most two
new seed-42, 25k-update delayed runs. A training-only Taylor diagnostic
chooses the smallest acceptable time from $\{1/4,1/2,3/4\}$, or stops the
experiment. Generated-force, geometric, coverage and stereochemical checks
are paired by validation molecule. Invalid physical computations remain
visible and cannot create a passing subset. The raw Hessian, not the
regularized $K$, is used for Taylor diagnostics.

If both delayed runs improve similarly, the observation supports a path
change but does not establish a benefit from curvature directions.
Even a directional difference-in-differences does not isolate timing from
signal strength. A single training seed cannot establish general benefit.
MMFF94-only improvement is not evidence of accuracy under another PES.

\paragraph{Prior art and implementation.}

(\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11433}); CurvFM studies curvature supervision
of generated structures (\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34592}). Source

(\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22388}). Novelty and performance remain
unestablished. Code lives in core \texttt{stifflow/research\_paths.py};
the original Alternative A label implementation and original archival
documents are unchanged. Float64 checks cover noncommuting factors,
endpoints, knot derivatives, symmetry, null spectra and the two integrals.
